Daily workflow

  1. Set up block and epidural kits for all first starts. If you have time, set up for the rest of the day to get ahead.
  2. Always check labs yourself. For epidurals: INR <1.4, PTT <40, PLT >100.
  3. Consent patients: written out of the OR, verbal in the OR.
  4. Put on monitors and oxygen. Nursing should, but often doesn't.
  5. Connect the device to the PACU spot.
  6. Tell the intraop anesthesia team you're there. Otherwise they can delay the block start. Exception: CRNA rooms, since CRNAs come in at 7.

PACU North arrivals

Keep track of blocks coming to PACU North from inpatient floors or 3W. When to call depends on how the current case in the room is going. Call for the patient early.

Remind the block nurse to send for the patient, and always ask whether the patient is ready from a surgical paperwork standpoint.

Learned plans

Each time a block is marked done, the board remembers the surgery, the blocks and the syringes. When someone types a similar surgery in "Here for", it suggests what the team did most often. Left and right are handled automatically.

Consenting

  • In the OR: verbal consent.
  • Out of the OR: written consent with all attending names listed. You can witness; the attending signs.
  • OR request, patient already asleep: call the family for phone consent. Use "+ OR request" on the board.
  • When consenting G1/G2 patients in the morning, also cover what the OR team would: NPO, allergies, etc.
  • Always assess for a pre-existing neuro deficit and document it.

Block space thoracic epidural workflow

    This checklist also appears on any patient with an epidural or CSE, where you can tick it off.

    PCEA orders

    • Standard: fentanyl 5 mcg/mL + bupivacaine 0.0625%. 4 mL/h continuous, 3 mL q10 min demand, 22 mL limit.
    • Cab: 3 mL q20 min, 3 boluses.
    • Oversedation: 2 mL/h, 2 mL q10 min, 1-hour limit 14 mL.
    • Dilaudid PCEA for esophagectomy: 4 / 3 / 10 / 22 (Dilaudid 10 mcg/mL only), plus a Dilaudid 500 mcg syringe.

    Nerve catheters

    Catheter type

    • Brachial plexus: Pajunk (less stiff).
    • To tunnel: Arrow.

    Block space nerve catheter workflow

      This checklist also appears on any patient with a peripheral block marked "Catheter".

      PCRA orders

      • Intermittent: ropivacaine 0.1% 4 mL q20 min.
      • Continuous: ropivacaine 0.2% 4–8 mL/h.
      • PIFB / PIRB: ropivacaine 0.1% 2 mL/h, 20 mL q240 min (PCA dose 0).

      Intrathecal catheters

      • Label it obviously.
      • Give a direct handoff with instructions.
      • Typical dosing 0.5 mL q2h. Leakage around the catheter or resolution of the block may call for more frequent dosing.

      Paravertebrals

      • Mick robotic MVR: highest concentration possible, usually 2 levels (sometimes 3: T3, T5, T7).
      • Bupivacaine + Decadron + epi.

      Peds blocks

      Peds epidural

      • 0.05 mL/kg per dermatome. See the dosing sheet; only fellows can order.
      • Depth to epidural space ≈ 1 mm/kg.
      • Catheter markings are 1 cm per line; needle markings are 0.5 cm per line.
      • Test dose: 0.1 mL/kg of 1:200,000.
      • Orders only by NP, fellows or attending.
      • Catheter is typically at loss + 3 cm.
      • Peds PCEA code: 025.

      Pudendal

      • Ropivacaine 0.2%. Can add Precedex for more complex blocks; see the guide in the anesthesia app.
      • Bump with sheets, then frog leg.
      • 50 mm block needle; ultrasound with the small probe.

      Ilioinguinal

      • Ropivacaine 0.2% plus Precedex.
      • Helpful for scrotoplasty with more posterior involvement.
      • Lots of gel on the bone. Point toward the belly button from the AIIS.
      • Usually enter medial to lateral.

      PIFB

      • 0.3–0.4 mL/kg per side, max 20 mL. The board shows this range when a syringe is linked to a PIFB.

      Peds dosing guideline

      Pediatric Regional Anesthesia – Local Anesthetic Dosing Guidelines, revised September 2025. These are proposed guidelines; adjust for multiple blocks, comorbidities and analgesic needs. Always stay within maximum safe doses.

      Key considerations

      • Ultrasound: as little as 0.1 mL/kg may give circumferential spread in extremity blocks. Use a small-footprint, high-frequency linear probe.
      • Physiology: lower minimum effective concentration, but potentially higher susceptibility to LAST.
      • Planning: use the WCMC Regional Dosing Chart. State the intended and max allowable volumes in the pre-anesthetic plan and at time-out. Consider continuous epidural or catheters for admitted patients with expected moderate to severe pain. Post-induction, pre-incision blocks help many patients, especially for short procedures.
      • Preferred agent: ropivacaine 0.2–0.5% (lower cardiotoxicity).
      • Dilution: two-provider dilutions are highly recommended. Example: ropivacaine 0.2% = 4 mL of 0.5% + 6 mL saline.

      Adjuncts

      Limited pediatric data. Default to plain local, and confirm with the attending first.

      Epinephrine1:400k–1:200k (2.5–5 mcg/mL). Marker for intravascular injection. Acral ischemia risk (fingers, toes, nose, pinna, penis) is largely debunked, but case reports exist in at-risk patients.
      Clonidine1 mcg/kg/block (max 2 mcg/kg)
      Dexmedetomidine0.5 mcg/kg/block (max 1 mcg/kg)
      DexamethasoneUnclear benefit over IV. Common dosing 0.1 mg/kg, max 4 mg. Infant: avoid. School-aged: 0.5 mg per block. Adolescent: 1 mg per block.

      CRPP

      • Supine, arms crossed.
      • No reaming or cementing.
      • Quick, so usually just a spinal.

      CMN / rod

      • Spinal if it's the first case.
      • Second IV if the first is in the AC, because the arms cross.
      • Supine.

      THA

      • PENG 15–20 mL max, because they do PT on POD 0.
      • Lateral.

      HHA

      • A-line, second IV.
      • Lateral.

      Olecranon ORIF

      • Prone for Dvo, so GETA. Sometimes supine or lateral.
      • Infraclavicular vs supraclavicular.
      • No need for T2 ICB for tourniquet pain, since they're under GA.
      • For Ricci: supine with the arm above the face on a Mayo stand.

      Distal femur fracture

      • GETA plus CSE for post-op pain if they plan the entire lateral incision.
      • Asks for paralysis.
      • Dvo is slow, so it becomes a 4+ hour case.

      Ricci: lateral humerus / elbow

      • Usually wants full paralysis, even supine (blocks plus GETA).
      • Lateral on a bean bag, operative arm in 90° suspension, axillary roll.
      • Down arm hyperflexed at the elbow to stay out of the field.
      • Get good non-AC access.
      • BP cuff on the leg if the arm position compresses the face.
      • Pad the bean bag where the hips are.

      Ankles

      • Supine: spinal plus analgesic blocks.
      • Prone: usually GA plus surgical-strength blocks if weight allows.
      • Popliteal and saphenous.

      Tibial plateau

      • GA, no blocks, because of concern for compartment syndrome (mainly a risk in acute fractures).
      • Sometimes a spinal.
      • Sometimes rescue femoral, iPACK or genicular blocks.
      • Consider methadone and ketamine; very painful.
